# LinkedIn Algorithm — How It Works (2024–2026)

## Overview

LinkedIn's algorithm prioritizes **meaningful professional conversations** over viral clickbait. The system evaluates content through a multi-phase distribution pipeline, rewarding posts that generate genuine engagement within professional communities.

---

## Distribution Phases

### Phase 1: Quality Filter (0–15 minutes)
- Post is classified as **spam**, **low-quality**, or **clear**
- Checked against known spam patterns, engagement-bait templates, and policy violations
- AI classifier scans for originality — copy-paste or heavily templated content is suppressed
- Posts with external links in the body are flagged for reduced initial distribution

### Phase 2: Test Audience (15 minutes – 2 hours)
- Shown to a small subset of your network (~8–12% of connections)
- Algorithm measures **early engagement velocity**:
  - Dwell time (time spent reading the post)
  - Reaction rate
  - Comment rate within first 60 minutes
  - Profile clicks from the post
- If engagement exceeds threshold → promoted to Phase 3
- If engagement is flat → distribution stalls permanently

### Phase 3: Extended Network (2–24 hours)
- Distributed beyond 1st-degree connections into 2nd-degree feeds
- **Comment quality** becomes the dominant signal:
  - Comments >15 words with relevant content boost distribution
  - One-word reactions ("Great!", "Love this") are nearly ignored
  - Back-and-forth threads (creator replies to commenters) are heavily rewarded
- **Share velocity**: reposts with commentary > silent reposts > no shares
- Hashtag relevance is evaluated — mismatched hashtags suppress distribution

### Phase 4: Viral / Sustained Distribution (24–72+ hours)
- Only ~2–5% of posts reach this phase
- Triggered by sustained engagement beyond the initial audience
- Content enters **topic feeds** and **LinkedIn News** consideration
- Algorithm checks for **engagement diversity** — engagement from multiple companies/industries signals broad relevance
- Posts can sustain distribution for 5–14 days if engagement continues

---

## Key Ranking Signals

### Dwell Time
- LinkedIn measures how long users pause on your post in their feed
- **Long-form posts** (800–1,300 characters) that are well-formatted earn higher dwell time
- Hook lines that stop the scroll are critical — first 2–3 lines visible before "...see more"
- Carousel documents generate highest dwell time per impression
- Dwell time is weighted more heavily than reactions since 2024

### Comment Quality
- The algorithm classifies comments by depth and relevance
- **High-value comments**: ask follow-up questions, share related experiences, respectfully disagree
- **Low-value comments**: generic praise, emoji-only, one-word responses
- Creator replies within the first 2 hours dramatically boost Phase 2→3 promotion
- Posts with 10+ substantive comments almost always break into Phase 3

### Share Velocity
- Reposts with added commentary count ~3x more than silent reposts
- DM shares (private) are tracked and contribute to distribution signals
- Share-to-impression ratio above 1.5% signals viral-tier content
- Shares from high-SSI (Social Selling Index) profiles carry more weight

### Profile Signals
- Creator's **historical engagement rate** affects initial distribution ceiling
- Consistent posting cadence (3–5x/week) increases baseline distribution
- Profile completeness and SSI score influence algorithmic trust
- Network quality matters — connections with high engagement rates boost your content

---

## Resurgence Triggers

Posts can experience a **second wave** of distribution after initial engagement slows:

1. **Late viral comment**: A high-profile user comments 24–48 hours after posting → algorithm re-evaluates and pushes to their network
2. **Topic trending**: If the post's topic becomes trending on LinkedIn, older relevant posts get redistributed
3. **Cross-platform inbound**: External traffic (from Twitter/X, newsletters, Slack communities) arriving at the post signals external validation
4. **Engagement chain reaction**: A comment thread goes deep (5+ replies) and generates its own engagement
5. **Creator activity**: If you engage meaningfully with comments 24–48 hours after posting, the algorithm treats the post as "still active"

## What Kills Reach

### External Links in Post Body
- Posts with links in the body receive **40–60% less distribution** than text-only posts
- LinkedIn wants users to stay on-platform
- **Mitigation**: Put links in the first comment, or use "link in comments" CTA
- Exception: LinkedIn article links and LinkedIn event links are not penalized

### Engagement Bait
- "Like if you agree, comment if you disagree" — actively penalized since 2024
- Poll posts designed for cheap engagement are suppressed
- "Follow me for more" CTAs in every post trigger spam classification
- Asking for reactions by type ("Give a 🔥 if...") is flagged

### Engagement Pods
- LinkedIn actively detects coordinated engagement groups
- Signals: same accounts engaging within minutes of every post, identical engagement patterns
- Penalty: reduced distribution ceiling for all pod participants
- Recovery: 4–8 weeks of organic-only engagement to restore algorithmic trust

### Content That Underperforms
- Purely personal content with no professional angle (vacation photos, family updates)
- Multi-image posts without context (album dumps)
- Overly promotional content (product launches without narrative)
- Content that reads like an AI-generated template without personal voice
- Posts longer than 3,000 characters (engagement drops after ~1,300 characters)
- Posting more than 2x per day (posts compete with each other)

### Editing After Posting
- Editing a post within the first hour can reset distribution momentum
- Minor typo fixes are usually safe; substantive content changes are penalized
- Best practice: proofread before posting, avoid edits in the first 4 hours

## Optimal Post Characteristics

### Format
- **Text posts** with strong hooks and line breaks perform best for engagement rate
- **Carousel documents** (PDF) generate highest dwell time and save rate
- **Native video** (under 90 seconds, with captions) gets preferential treatment in feed
- **Polls** generate high engagement but low-quality engagement — use sparingly
- **LinkedIn articles** (long-form) get separate distribution through newsletter and SEO

### Structure
- **Hook** (lines 1–2): Pattern interrupt, contrarian take, or curiosity gap — this is what shows before "see more"
- **Body** (lines 3–10): Story, framework, or insight with white space and line breaks
- **CTA** (last 1–2 lines): Specific question that invites meaningful comments
- Optimal length: **800–1,300 characters** for text posts
- Use line breaks every 1–2 sentences — wall-of-text posts get scrolled past

### Timing
- **Tuesday–Thursday**: highest engagement days
- **7:30–8:30 AM** in target audience timezone: peak feed-checking time
- **12:00–1:00 PM**: secondary peak during lunch breaks
- **Avoid**: weekends (30–50% lower distribution), Friday afternoons, Monday mornings before 8 AM
- Consistency matters more than perfect timing — pick a schedule and maintain it

### Hashtags
- Use **3–5 relevant hashtags** maximum
- Mix: 1–2 broad (#leadership, #productmanagement), 2–3 niche (#pmlife, #startupfounder)
- Hashtags with 10K–500K followers perform best — avoid mega-hashtags (#innovation, 30M+ followers) where your post drowns
- Place hashtags at the end of the post, not inline

### Engagement Strategy Post-Publish
- Reply to every comment within the first 2 hours
- Ask follow-up questions in replies to extend comment threads
- Engage with 5–10 posts from your network before and after publishing (primes the algorithm to show your content to those people)
- Do not share your own post immediately — wait 30+ minutes
